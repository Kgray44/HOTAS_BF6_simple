18. Auto and Alpha-Beta-Gamma were effectively tied in the focused synthetic controls; Alpha-Beta and Velocity were modestly less accurate. At 30 ms every direct-model control had tail findings, so a narrow control-model win is not an architecture conclusion.
19. The horizon sweep was monotonic rather than a single numerical cliff: mean MAE rose from 0.002300 at 8 ms to 0.011914 at 30 ms and 0.025464 at 50 ms; harmful rows rose from 0/70 at 8 ms to 23/70 at 30 ms and 30/70 at 50 ms.
20. At 30 ms, 250 Hz was the best tested source-rate aggregate (MAE 0.008955; 4 harmful rows) but still had tail findings; 60 Hz was worst (0.014207; 5 harmful rows). Source cadence shifts the envelope but does not remove the high-horizon risk.
21. Reversal evidence is the principal weakness: Canonical reproduced `reversal/pattern-73`, where a substantive human reversal is conveyed by the device at approximately 308 ms but is not detected/reacquired. It appears across all nine active models/presets, for 18 `PRODUCT_SUSPECT` observations. This is a `MODEL_DEFECT_CANDIDATE`, not a confirmed defect and not repaired here.
22. Stop/settling remained bounded: no NaN, infinite, illegal-output, or persistent-stationary-offset condition was found. Fast physical-stop outliers were retained rather than removed.
23. Micro-adjustment/randomized-human behavior was stable in the 100-trajectory randomized corpus: zero harmful rows across its 1,000 production-configuration replays. Canonical’s precision timing classifications remain mostly HARNESS-policy observations, not a product confirmation.
24. Endpoint approaches taper safely within legal bounds. At long horizons the useful lead compresses near endpoints while tail future-position error grows, especially on rapid full sweeps.
25. Noise tests did not create a hard correctness failure. The stationary-jitter trace is retained; higher presets produce larger predictor-only steps, so it remains a Phase 2 constraint.
26. Randomized human-like results agree with deterministic results below the low-teens horizon range and did not expose a new hard failure.
27. Physical results disagree with synthetic averages at the high end: larger horizons have materially worse tails on `fast-full-sweep-1` and `very-fast-full-sweep-1`. This is the reason the recorded corpus limits the recommendation rather than being treated as another optimization target.
28. Representative outliers are preserved in `physical_per_trace_results.csv`: Fast on `very-fast-full-sweep-1` had MAE 0.051642 / p95 0.156760, and Auto 30 ms / 20% lead had MAE 0.164094 / p95 0.478998 on that same trace. These are valid real-human-motion results, not discarded data.

## 35–45. Envelope, Phase 2 recommendation, and conclusion

35. Determined operating envelope:

| Region | Horizon | Character |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Conservative | 0–8 ms | Stable across deterministic validation; Light (4 ms) was clean across all 15 physical traces. |
| General-use | 8–10 ms | Strong synthetic fidelity; confirm tails against any eventual optimization candidate. |
| Aggressive | 12–18 ms | Useful lead remains, but reversal/physical-sweep tail evidence grows. |
| Conditional | 20–24 ms | Requires motion-family/rate gating; not generally safe. |
| Experimental | 30 ms | Has use on coherent, slower motion but is not broadly justified by the real traces. |
| Failure territory | 35–50 ms | Monotonically increasing tail error; 50 ms has no general-production case. |

36. The practical knee is approximately 12–18 ms; degradation then continues smoothly through 50 ms. It is sharper for fast sweeps, stops/reversals, physical-corpus timing, and lower source rates.
37. 30 ms is conditionally useful for coherent synthetic movement, but its physical tail evidence does not justify a general default.
38. 35–50 ms yielded no legitimate general-use region; retain only sparse regression probes, not a broad Phase 2 search.
39. Current presets occupy increasing aggressiveness regions. Light is the only production preset with zero harmful physical-trace rows under this fixed classification; Extreme is dominated for general use by its larger tail cost. This is characterization, not a request to alter presets.
40. Highest-influence parameters/interactions: horizon, motion family/speed, reversal/stop timing, and source cadence. Maximum lead interacts with horizon; it should not be tuned independently.
41. Lower-influence parameters for initial search: direct predictor-model choice within the Auto/ABG control family and modest high-rate cadence variation. Keep them as controls, not primary search dimensions.
42. Recommended bounded Phase 2 search space: horizon 2–24 ms (dense 2 ms samples through 12 ms; 3–4 ms boundary samples thereafter); maximum lead 6–15% with joint horizon/lead sampling; Auto as the production model; retain 30–50 ms only as fixed regression sentinels. Do not use the holdout or repeatedly fit all 15 physical traces.
43. Independent validation reproduced the deterministic trend: 8 ms had zero harmful rows; 18, 24, 30, 35, and 40 ms had 16, 21, 23, 25, and 25 harmful rows respectively out of 70.
44. The 100-scenario holdout hash is recorded above and was generated but not replayed for configuration conclusions.
45. Final recommendation: open a separate, explicitly authorized product investigation for the reproduced reversal candidate before preset optimization. If that investigation rules it non-product or resolves it, Phase 2 should use the bounded search space above and preserve the physical traces as evaluation evidence.
